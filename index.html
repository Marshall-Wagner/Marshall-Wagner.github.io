Enterprise 802.1X / RADIUS Authentication

Deployed enterprise wireless authentication on the WW Machining staff Wi-Fi, replacing a shared Wi-Fi passphrase with per-user 802.1X. Stood up Windows Server 2025 with Active Directory, AD Certificate Services (Enterprise Root CA), and Network Policy Server (NPS) as the RADIUS server; issued the NPS server certificate from the internal CA and configured PEAP-MSCHAPv2 against Active Directory, with RADIUS accounting and authentication logging enabled on NPS for visibility into every auth attempt. An internal-only Certificate Authority Web Enrollment portal (HTTPS certsrv, hosted on IIS), reachable only from devices already on the LAN/Wi-Fi, is used mainly for self-service certificate renewal by already-authorized devices rather than new-device enrollment (a new device cannot reach the portal until it is already on the network); domain-joined machines trust the Enterprise Root CA automatically via Group Policy. The domain controller hosts the AD DNS zone and forwards external queries to AdGuard Home, so domain-joined machines keep network-wide filtering and encrypted DNS. Staff devices authenticate over a WPA2-Enterprise (AES) access point on the 5 GHz SSID with a per-AP random RADIUS shared secret, while the WPA2-only IoT cameras stay isolated on a separate 2.4 GHz PSK SSID. The 802.1X model authenticates each user with their own AD credentials, giving per-user accountability and per-user revocation instead of a shared Wi-Fi password. Proved PEAP-MSCHAPv2 end-to-end from a Linux client with eapol_test before cutting over real devices. The open-source equivalent (FreeRADIUS with a MariaDB / MySQL-compatible backend) was also built, covering both the Microsoft (NPS/AD) and open-source 802.1X RADIUS stacks. The domain controller / NPS runs on UPS-protected power (battery backup, AVR voltage regulation, EMI/RFI filtering) so a brownout or outage on the shop floor doesn’t drop company-wide Wi-Fi authentication.

Self-Hosted Video Surveillance with AI Person Detection

Deployed a self-hosted NVR for after-hours monitoring of the WW Machining office: a 5 MP PoE dome camera feeding Frigate for AI person detection with 24/7 continuous recording. The camera is powered over PoE by a repurposed 802.3af/at injector reused from a retired 5G modem rather than new hardware, and secured with a randomly generated 20-character LUDS password to resist brute-force attacks. Frigate pulls two RTSP streams from the camera: a low-resolution H.264 sub-stream (640x480 at 5 fps) for detection and the full-resolution H.265 main stream (2560x1920 at 25 fps) for recording. The camera ships RTSP and ONVIF disabled by default (only its proprietary app port exposed), so both were enabled, and HTTPS was enabled on the camera with a self-signed certificate for the Home Assistant integration. Recording is 24/7 continuous as a safety net so nothing is missed if a detection does not trigger; retention is tiered - all footage is kept 3 days, motion-detection events 7 days, and person-detection alerts 14 days. Frigate is also capped to 4 CPU threads so its detection workload doesn’t starve the other self-hosted services sharing the server. Frigate records the camera’s native H.265 stream directly to disk without re-encoding, with live and event recordings, snapshots, and clips written to a dedicated data SSD separate from the OS drive. The camera is denied outbound internet access at the X-Wrt firewall (its WAN-bound traffic is rejected), so it cannot phone home and Frigate pulls the stream entirely on the LAN. The Frigate web UI is reverse-proxied through Caddy with a real Let’s Encrypt TLS certificate and restricted to the LAN and Wireguard VPN, and Home Assistant provides a live camera dashboard and camera firmware-update monitoring (patch-hygiene visibility). The Home Assistant UI is hardened the same way as the rest of the stack: reverse-proxied through Caddy with a Let’s Encrypt certificate, restricted to the LAN and Wireguard VPN with remote access disabled, and protected by a 64-character LUDS password. The result is self-hosted video surveillance with 24/7 recording, AI person detection, and tiered retention - no cloud or subscription dependency.
